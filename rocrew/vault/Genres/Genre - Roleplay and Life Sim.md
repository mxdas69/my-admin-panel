# Genre - Roleplay and Life Sim

Roleplay (RP) and life-sim games give players a place, a role and props, and let them make up the story themselves: a town with houses and cars, a school with classes, a family with a baby and a pet, a job with a uniform and a counter, a dream house to build and decorate. The players make the content by playing pretend together. The designer's job is to supply believable places, useful props, clear roles and light structure, and then get out of the way. A 2026 genre roundup puts roleplay at the top of Roblox categories by visits [2]; treat that as a direction, not a number, since it is a third-party ranking.

This chapter covers four shapes: **town RP** (an open town where everyone picks a role, a house and a vehicle, the Brookhaven shape), **structured RP** (jobs, teams and duties with light rules: police, hospital, pizza place, school), **life sim** (needs, money, jobs and a house that you build and keep, the Bloxburg shape), and **pet and family sim** (raise pets or babies through care tasks, collect and trade, the Adopt Me shape). Most hits mix two of these.

RP games win or lose on three things: **the first minute** (does a new player get a house, a car and an outfit fast enough to start playing pretend?), **the props** (are there enough things to *do* with friends in each place?), and **safety** (is all text filtered, are houses lockable, is griefing hard?). They also carry the highest moderation risk of any genre, because players create the scenes. Reviewer must read section 11 and section 13 before any RP build ships.

Read this page before pitching or planning an RP or life-sim game, and before building houses, vehicles, jobs, name tags, furniture placement or avatar editors in any genre.

## 1. What it is and why it works on Roblox

**The audience.** RP is where many younger players spend time with friends after school: they act out family life, school, jobs, emergencies and adventures with people they know. Our design assumption is a core of 8-13 year-olds who play in groups of 2-5 friends, plus solo players who join to meet people. They value self-expression (outfits, houses, names), belonging (a family, a team, a friend's house) and things to show off (rare pets, a big house, a cool car).

**Session shape.** Long and loose. A session is "join friends → set up (house, outfit, role) → play scenes → change scene → leave". Design targets for this studio: setup under 60 s for a new player and under 15 s for a returning one; sessions of 30-90 minutes; 3-6 scene changes per session (home, school, shop, hospital, park, beach).

**Why players come back.**
1. **Their friends are there.** RP is social glue. The game is the playground.
2. **Their stuff is saved.** A decorated house, a pet that ages, an outfit closet, earned money.
3. **Growth they care about.** A pet growing up, a bigger house, a rarer item, a new job rank.
4. **New props and places.** Updates add a new building, vehicle or item set, which means new scenes to play.
5. **Status.** Rare items and big houses are visible to everyone in the server.

**Why it works on Roblox.** Roblox's avatars, chat, friends, private servers and free multiplayer are exactly what RP needs, and Roblox's genre settings let creators place their game in a genre and subgenre so it reaches the right players [1]. RP games are also forgiving of rough mechanics: a town with good props and a smooth first minute can thrive with no combat, no levels and almost no tutorial.

## 2. The core loop

**Town RP loop:**
> Players **pick a role, a house and a vehicle**, **act out scenes with friends using the town's props**, and **unlock new props, houses and vehicles** so they can **play bigger and stranger scenes**.

**Structured RP loop:**
> Players **take a job or team role**, **do its tasks for other players** (serve, heal, arrest, teach), **earn pay and rank**, and **unlock better gear, vehicles and authority**.

**Life sim loop:**
> Players **work jobs and care for their character's needs** to **earn money**, which they spend on **building and furnishing their own house**, so they can **show it off and host friends**.

**Pet and family sim loop:**
> Players **care for a pet or baby through tasks** to **earn money and age it up**, which they spend on **eggs, items and house upgrades**, so they can **collect, combine and trade rarer pets**.

| Layer | Time scale | Town RP | Life sim | Pet sim |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Micro | 1-10 s | Sit, drive, emote, use a prop | Do one job action | Feed, play, tap a task |
| Short | 1-5 min | One scene ("the baby is sick → hospital") | One job shift, one purchase | One task cycle, money paid, age XP |
| Session | 30-90 min | Several scenes, a new outfit, a house redesign | A room built, a promotion | A pet aged up, an egg hatched, a trade |
| Long | weeks | New props each update, a group of RP friends | The dream house | A full collection, rare combined pets |

Loop rules:
1. **Every place needs at least 3 things to do with your hands.** A hospital has beds you can lie in, an X-ray you can trigger, a check-in desk with a working bell. A building without interactions is a backdrop, not a scene.
2. **Roles must change something.** A "Police" role that only changes a name tag gets abandoned; give it a uniform, a car with a siren, a badge prompt and a jail door.
3. **Money is optional in town RP and central in life sim.** Pick one: free props for everyone (town RP, monetized through passes) or earned props (life sim, monetized through time savers and cosmetics). Mixing both badly gives players nothing to work for.
4. **Care tasks pay on completion, not by the minute.** A pet sim pays for each finished task so AFK players can't farm (rule of thumb).

## 3. Top games in the genre

> **Source status.** This page's research pass hit the session's web-search limit before the game-specific searches. Entries marked **†** describe the game from the crew's general knowledge, with no citation yet. Before you quote any detail from a † entry as fact, verify it in-game or find a source. None of these entries gives player counts, revenue or other numbers, and none should until it is sourced.

### Brookhaven RP †
**What it does:** A free town RP: players pick a house from a row of houses, spawn vehicles from a menu, change outfits and names, and play pretend across a town with a school, hospital, police station, shops and homes. Extra houses, vehicles and abilities are sold as game passes rather than earned through a grind.
**Steal:** Instant setup (house and car within seconds), zero grind, a dense town where every building has props, and passes that add toys rather than gating basic play.
**Avoid:** Copying its town layout, buildings or UI. Its success depends on years of polish and a huge audience; a thin clone with fewer props loses.

### Adopt Me! †
**What it does:** A pet and family sim: players adopt and raise pets (and play as babies or parents), complete care tasks (hunger, thirst, sleep and similar needs) that pay currency and age the pet through stages, hatch eggs, decorate a house, and trade. Combining several fully grown copies of the same pet makes a glowing "Neon" version, and limited event pets give collections a time dimension.
**Steal:** Care tasks as the core verb, ageing as visible progress, combining duplicates into a better version, a trading economy, and limited items tied to events.
**Avoid:** A trading economy without anti-scam protections (a two-sided confirm screen with a countdown) or with paid random items whose odds aren't shown ([[03 Rarity and RNG]]).

### Welcome to Bloxburg †
**What it does:** A life sim centred on a detailed build mode (walls, floors, furniture, landscaping), jobs that pay in-game money (cashier, delivery and similar), and character needs. Players build a home, work, and host friends.
**Steal:** A build mode deep enough to be the game on its own, and jobs that are short real minigames rather than idle timers.
**Avoid:** A build mode that's unusable on a phone. Grid snapping, big handles and an undo button are mandatory.

### Work at a Pizza Place †
**What it does:** Structured job RP: players pick a station (cashier, cook, boxer, delivery, supplier, manager) in a shared pizza restaurant, and each station's work feeds the next; pay buys furniture for a personal house.
**Steal:** Interlocking jobs where one player's work is another's input (orders → cooking → boxing → delivery), which creates cooperation without rules text.
**Avoid:** A station that's boring alone; each job must work with 1 player and be better with 3.

### Royale High †
**What it does:** A fantasy school RP mixed with dress-up: classes and activities earn currency for a large wardrobe of outfits and accessories, with seasonal events and limited items.
**Steal:** School as a structure (a bell schedule gives the server a rhythm), dress-up as the reward, seasonal sets.
**Avoid:** An economy so slow that the only real path is buying currency.

### MeepCity †
**What it does:** A social hangout town with a pet companion (the "Meep"), a personal house to decorate, and casual activities (fishing, a party place, a school) as conversation starters.
**Steal:** Activities as excuses to hang out; a companion that's an always-on cosmetic.
**Avoid:** Thin activities that get old in one session.

### Berry Avenue †
**What it does:** A town RP that leans hard on avatar customization: players build outfits in an in-game editor and share outfit codes, alongside houses, vehicles and a town.
**Steal:** Outfit sharing (codes or a gallery) turns players into content creators for each other.
**Avoid:** An avatar editor that allows unmoderated text or images.

### Emergency Response: Liberty County †
**What it does:** A structured city RP with police, fire, EMS, DOT and civilian/criminal roles, vehicles with lights and sirens, and calls that give roles something to respond to. Community servers often add their own rules.
**Steal:** Calls and incidents generated by the game so emergency roles always have work, even in a quiet server.
**Avoid:** Role systems that require a strict moderator to be fun; build the structure into the game.

### Private servers as an RP product (pattern) — sourced
**What it does:** Roblox games sell private servers as a monthly subscription; Tower of Hell, for example, charges 250 Robux per month and adds extra settings for the owner [15]. RP groups use private servers for planned scenes with friends.
**Steal:** A private server with owner tools (lock the server to friends, set the time of day, toggle events, kick) is the best-fitting monthly product in RP.
**Avoid:** Making public servers worse to push private ones.

## 4. The first five minutes, second by second

| Time | What the player sees and does | Build note |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3 s | Spawns in a bright plaza with the town visible in three directions. A short "Pick your look" panel slides in from the right with 6 preset outfits and "Keep my avatar". | Preset outfits are HumanoidDescriptions on the server; "Keep" is the default. |
| 3-10 s | Panel 2: "Pick a role": Citizen, Student, Parent, Baby, Doctor, Police, Chef, Teacher (icons, no text walls). One tap. | Roles are Teams or attributes; picking sets a uniform and a name-tag color. |
| 10-20 s | Panel 3: "Get a house": a map pin row of free houses. Tap one → teleport to its door, door opens, "This is your house!" | Claim with a ProximityPrompt or the panel; one house per player. |
| 20-40 s | Inside: furniture with prompts (bed: "Sleep", fridge: "Grab snack", TV: "Watch"). A phone button pulses on the HUD. | 3+ interactions in the first room. |
| 40-70 s | Phone opens: Vehicles, Outfits, Houses, Jobs, Friends. "Spawn a car" puts a car at the driveway. | One vehicle per player, spawned within 12 studs of the player. |
| 70-150 s | Drive to a landmark the phone points to (school, hospital, beach). An activity prompt there ("Start class", "Check in") gives the first scene. | Sight lines from the house to two landmarks [17]. |
| 150-300 s | First earned or unlocked thing (life sim: first paycheck; pet sim: first task paid; town RP: a free item from the shop). A friend-join hint: "Invite friends to your house". | First reward under 3 minutes. |

Opening rules:
1. **Setup under 60 s, skippable.** A returning player skips straight to their saved house (spawn at the door).
2. **Three taps, not three paragraphs.** Kids skip text. Use big icons with one-word labels.
3. **No paywall in setup.** Paid houses and vehicles appear in the list with a pass icon, but at least 6 houses and 6 vehicles are free.
4. **A scene starter is always on screen.** A small card ("Try: take the baby to the hospital") gives players with no ideas a place to start.

## 5. Progression and economy

### Shapes
- **Town RP (no grind):** no currency or a token currency. Progression is collection (props, outfits, vehicles) and social. Monetization: passes.
- **Life sim (wage economy):** money from jobs; spend on house plots, rooms, furniture, vehicles. Progression: house value and job rank.
- **Pet sim (care economy):** money from care tasks; spend on eggs and items; pets age through stages; duplicates combine.

### Life sim wages (targets)
Let the first useful purchase (a bed, a better car) cost about 10 minutes of work, the first room expansion 1 hour, and a "dream house" 15-30 hours, with jobs ranking up to raise pay.

| Job rank | Pay per completed task | Tasks per minute (skilled) | Money per hour | Rank up after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Trainee | 8 | 4 | 1,920 | 50 tasks |
| 2 Worker | 10 | 4.5 | 2,700 | 150 tasks |
| 3 Senior | 13 | 5 | 3,900 | 400 tasks |
| 4 Lead | 16 | 5 | 4,800 | 1,000 tasks |
| 5 Manager | 20 | 5 | 6,000 | — |

Prices then follow from hours: a bed 800 (about 25 minutes at rank 1), a car 6,000, a second floor 15,000, a luxury plot 120,000. Balance simulates rank-up timing in `sim/` and checks that a player who works 30 minutes a day reaches Rank 3 in about a week.

### Needs (life sim, optional)
Needs give a reason to use the house: Hunger, Energy, Hygiene, Fun, each 0-100. Decay targets: Hunger −1 per 30 s, Energy −1 per 40 s, Hygiene −1 per 50 s, Fun −1 per 25 s, so a need reaches 0 in 40-80 minutes if ignored. Low needs (< 20) reduce job pay by 25%. They never kill, punish hard or block play. Restore rates: eating +40, sleeping +2/s, shower +5/s, TV +1/s.

### Pet care economy (targets)
- A task (feed, play, bath, sleep, school) appears every 60-90 s per pet, takes 10-30 s, and pays 20-35 money and 1 age point.
- Age stages: 6 stages at 0, 20, 50, 100, 170, 260 age points (about 4-6 hours of active care to full grown).
- Eggs: 750 (common pool) to 3,500 (rare pool); odds shown on the egg stand ([[03 Rarity and RNG]]).
- Combining: 4 full-grown duplicates → a glowing variant; 4 glowing → a top variant. This gives every duplicate a use.
- Trading: both sides confirm, then a 5 s countdown, then a final confirm. Value hints come from the game, never from players.

### Inflation guard
Money sources must have sinks: furniture, house expansions, eggs, vehicle colors, a repaint fee, event items. Track total money created vs. spent per day (Liveops); if the server-wide net is positive for 2+ weeks, add a sink rather than cutting wages.

## 6. Monetization that fits

RP players pay for **more self-expression** (houses, vehicles, outfits, effects), **more toys** (abilities, props, pets), **more control** (private servers, house permissions) and, in life sims, **time** (money packs, faster wages). Use the developer-product pipeline correctly: Roblox requires `ProcessReceipt` to grant developer products and warns against granting from `PromptProductPurchaseFinished` [8], the callback can be set only once by one server script [9], and a second script setting it can charge players without granting the item [10]; `ProcessReceipt` must be defined for developer-product transactions to complete [11]. DevForum creators also advise putting offers where players will see them and advertising things players can buy as they progress, rather than hiding everything in one shop [13][14].

Price ladder (targets; the owner sets real prices and creates passes and products):

| Item | Type | Target price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Premium house pack (3-5 houses) | Game pass | 199-399 R$ | Bigger, themed houses; free houses stay good |
| Vehicle pack | Game pass | 149-299 R$ | Sports cars, a limo, a bus, a helicopter |
| VIP (chat tag, VIP lounge, 1.5× pay) | Game pass | 249-499 R$ | Lounge is a hangout, not a power advantage |
| Abilities (fly, shrink, giant, invisible in your own house) | Game pass | 99-249 R$ each | Never usable to grief others (shrink can't enter locked houses) |
| Extra outfit slots (+10) | Game pass | 49-99 R$ | Cheap, high-volume |
| Money pack (life sim) | Developer product | 49 / 129 / 399 / 999 R$ | Show the amount in hours of work ("= 2 h of work") |
| Pet potion / instant age-up (pet sim) | Developer product | 49-199 R$ | One stage, not straight to full grown |
| Premium egg (pet sim) | Developer product | 99-299 R$ | Odds visible before purchase |
| Private server | Subscription | 100-250 R$/month | Owner tools; Tower of Hell's is 250 R$/month as a reference [15] |

Off-limits:
1. **No paid features that involve dating, romance or relationships.** RP servers attract that roleplay and Roblox's rules prohibit it; nothing we sell may suggest it (no "marry" items, no couple passes).
2. **No pay-to-grief.** Paid abilities never let someone trap, fling, follow-kill or enter a locked house.
3. **No random paid items without visible odds.**
4. **No pass that makes free houses or vehicles worse.**
5. **No "limited time" countdown that resets.**
6. **No paid advantage in trades** (no paid "trade boost" that hides value).

## 7. UI and HUD for this genre

The RP HUD centers on a **phone**: one button that opens every system. Players already understand a phone, and it keeps the screen clear for scenes.

| Element | Position (UDim2 / AnchorPoint) | Size (offset at 1080p) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phone button | (1, −16, 1, −180) / (1, 1) | 72×72 | Above the jump button; opens the phone |
| Phone panel | (1, −16, 0.5, 0) / (1, 0.5) | 360×640 (phone: 60% screen height) | App grid 3×3: Vehicles, Houses, Outfits, Jobs, Roles, Emotes, Friends, Settings, Shop |
| Money (life sim) | (0, 16, 0, 72) / (0, 0) | 200×48 | Green pill with icon; animates on change |
| Needs bars (life sim) | (0, 16, 0, 128) / (0, 0) | 4 × 160×18 | Icons + bars; hidden in town RP |
| Scene starter card | (0.5, 0, 0, 72) / (0.5, 0) | 360×56 | "Try: open the café" with a dismiss X; hides after 3 dismissals |
| Name tag (in world) | BillboardGui on Head | 200×50 studs-scaled, MaxDistance 60 | Filtered RP name + role color |
| Emote wheel | center | 420×420 | 8 slots; hold to open on PC (key G), button on mobile |
| Build mode bar | bottom center | 600×96 | Category tabs, item strip, rotate, move, delete, undo, exit |

Must-have screens:
1. **Role picker** (8-12 roles with icons and colors).
2. **House picker** (free and pass houses; "Lock", "Permissions", "Kick" for owners).
3. **Vehicle spawner** (cards with a color picker; "Despawn").
4. **Outfit/avatar editor** (presets, saved outfits, color pickers; filtered names).
5. **RP name and bio editor** (filtered, max 20 and 60 characters).
6. **Build mode** (life sim): grid snap 1 stud (toggle 0.25), rotate 15°/45°/90°, move, duplicate, delete, undo (20 steps), cost preview, "can't place" red outline.
7. **Trading** (pet sim): two inventories side by side, both confirm, 5 s countdown, final confirm.
8. **Settings**: hide name tags, music, vehicle sounds, low detail, block a player.

Mobile rules: the phone button and emote button sit in the right column above the jump button; nothing in the bottom-left thumbstick zone (300×300 px); build mode on phones uses a "tap to select, drag on grid, buttons to rotate" scheme, never right-click or keyboard shortcuts ([[08 Mobile]]). Gamepad: every menu button is selectable (`Selectable = true`, sensible `NextSelection*` links, `GuiService.SelectedObject` set when a menu opens) and ProximityPrompts keep their default gamepad key (`GamepadKeyCode`, ButtonX); build mode rotates with the shoulder buttons and confirms with ButtonA.

## 8. World and look

1. **A town is a set of stages.** Every landmark is a scene: Home, School, Hospital, Police, Fire Station, Café/Restaurant, Shop/Mall, Park, Beach, Gas Station, Hotel. Start with 6 and add one per update.
2. **Readable at street level.** Each landmark has a unique silhouette and color and a big sign visible from 150 studs. Landmarks and sight lines guide players [17]: from every house, at least two landmarks are visible.
3. **Scale.** Roads 28-36 studs wide (two lanes and sidewalks), sidewalks 8 studs, standard doors 5×8 studs (characters are about 5 studs tall), ceilings 12-14 studs, house plots 64×64 to 96×96 studs. Interiors are slightly oversized (rooms 20×20 or larger) so cameras don't clip.
4. **Interiors are the content.** Every building has a furnished interior with 3+ interactions per room.
5. **Day/night.** A 20-minute day (14 day, 6 night) with streetlights that turn on at dusk; private-server owners can lock the time.
6. **Art direction.** Clean, bright, slightly stylized; one palette for the town (warm pastels or bright primaries). Kids read clean shapes better than realism on phones ([[06 World and Look]]).
7. **Performance.** RP towns are big: `StreamingEnabled = true`; interiors as separate Models; vehicles limited to 1 per player and despawned when idle 5 minutes; furniture capped per house (400 items); `CastShadow = false` on small props; avoid many moving parts.
8. **Plan before you build.** Decide the genre, theme and level of detail before opening Studio, and lay out the town so something interesting is always in view [17].

## 9. Retention and social

1. **Saved house** that loads in under 3 s when you claim it, with furniture placed exactly as you left it.
2. **Friends' houses.** Show "Your friend Sam is home" with a "Visit" button; house owners can set "Friends only".
3. **Daily login gift** (money, an item, a pet treat), with a 7-day streak item ([[10 Retention and Social]]).
4. **Pets that need you.** Pets keep a gentle mood but never suffer or die; returning gives a warm greeting, not guilt.
5. **Collections.** An index of items, outfits, pets, achievements.
6. **Group perks:** a free item or house color for joining the group (`Player:IsInGroup`).
7. **Events.** Seasonal town decorations, an event currency and limited items.
8. **Invite flow:** "Invite friends to your house" uses the Roblox invite prompt.
9. **Safety is retention.** Parents and kids leave games where they get harassed; block, house lock, kick and report must work.

## 10. Live ops and update cadence

| Cadence | Content |
|---|---|
| Weekly | 10-20 new props or furniture, 1-2 vehicles or outfits, a bug-fix pass |
| Every 2-4 weeks | A new building or area (a new scene), a new job or role |
| Seasonal (4-6 per year) | Event: town decorations, event currency, 6-12 limited items, an event activity |
| Quarterly | A system (pets, build mode upgrade, trading, a new town district) |

Rules:
1. **New places beat new numbers.** A new building gives a new scene; a new currency gives nothing to play.
2. **Limited items must stay limited** after the event (no surprise re-releases that crash trade values) unless announced as a re-release.
3. **Never move a building players have built routines around** without a big announcement; regulars use it as a meeting point.
4. **Watch the setup funnel**: role picked → house claimed → vehicle spawned → first interaction. Any step losing 20%+ of players is next week's fix.
5. **Update board in the plaza**, plus the update name in the title for a week ([[12 Live Ops and Launch]]).

## 11. Building it in Studio

### Build order (a town RP in 14 steps)
1. **Architect** writes the setting, the 6 launch places, the role list (8-12), the house and vehicle lists (free and pass), and the shape (town, structured, life sim, pet sim) into `plan.md`, plus the safety plan for Reviewer.
2. **Balance** writes wages, prices and need rates in `economy.md` (life sim and pet sim only) and simulates a week of 30-minute sessions.
3. **Builder** writes `Config`, `PlayerData`, `RoleService` and `NameTagService` (with filtering) first: safety before features.
4. **Scenic** greyboxes the town: plaza, roads, plots and the 6 landmark shells, with sight lines from every plot to two landmarks.
5. **Builder** writes `HouseService` (claim, lock, sweep, save/load) and `VehicleService`; **Playtest** claims, locks, rejoins and checks layouts.
6. **Librarian** gathers furniture and props for the first room of each landmark (3+ interactions per room), with every model's scripts read.
7. **Builder** writes `InteractionService` and binds prompts by tag; **Scenic** places props.
8. **Frame** builds the phone, the role, house and vehicle pickers, the name editor and settings.
9. **Kid Tester** runs setup on a phone: look → role → house → car in under 60 s.
10. **Builder** adds jobs, needs, build mode or pets, depending on the shape.
11. **Merchant** wires passes, products and `ProcessReceipt`.
12. **Scenic** finishes interiors and lighting; day/night on.
13. **Reviewer** tests filtering, forged remotes (placement, money, trades), house sweeps and griefing paths.
14. **Producer** runs the section 15 checklist with evidence.

### Systems list
1. **PlayerData** (profile load/save with session locking; autosave 60 s).
2. **RoleService** (Teams or role attributes, uniforms, name-tag colors, role limits like "max 4 police").
3. **NameTagService** (RP name and bio; **filtered** text on BillboardGuis).
4. **HouseService** (plots, claim, lock, permissions, kick, load/save layouts).
5. **BuildService** (life sim: validate placements, costs, layout serialization).
6. **VehicleService** (spawn/despawn, one per player, idle cleanup, colors).
7. **InteractionService** (ProximityPrompts on furniture and props: sit, sleep, eat, use).
8. **JobService** (task generation, completion checks, pay, rank).
9. **NeedsService** (life sim: decay ticks, restore actions).
10. **PetService** (pet sim: spawn, follow, tasks, ageing, combining).
11. **TradeService** (pet sim: two-sided, server-held trade state).
12. **EconomyService** (Merchant: passes, products, `ProcessReceipt`).
13. **DayNightService** (`Lighting.ClockTime`).
14. **ModerationTools** (block, house kick, server kick for private-server owners, report hooks).

### Architecture
```
ReplicatedStorage/
  Shared/Config          roles, house list, vehicle list, prices, job pay table, need rates
  Shared/Catalog         furniture & item definitions: id, name, price, size, category
  Remotes/               ...see contract below
  Assets/Furniture/      furniture Models (Librarian), each with PrimaryPart and a "Footprint" size attribute
ServerStorage/
  Vehicles/              vehicle Models with a VehicleSeat
  Uniforms/              role clothing / accessories
ServerScriptService/Main + Services/ (one per system above)
Workspace/Map/
  Town/                  roads, landmarks
  Plots/Plot_01..Plot_24 each a Model tagged "Plot" with a ClaimPrompt, Door, Bounds part
StarterPlayer/StarterPlayerScripts/Controllers/
  PhoneController, BuildController, VehicleController, EmoteController, NameTagController
```

Interactions use ProximityPrompts: their visibility depends on a distance setting, line of sight (on by default) and an exclusivity setting, and you can handle them per prompt or globally through ProximityPromptService [3][4]. Use `MaxActivationDistance` 8-10 studs for furniture, `HoldDuration` 0 for sit/use and 0.5-1 s for "Claim house" or "Lock door" so taps don't trigger by accident, and `RequiresLineOfSight = true` so prompts don't fire through walls. Tag props with CollectionService and bind behavior once per tag, the same pattern used for obby kill parts [16].

Roles map naturally onto Teams: a non-neutral SpawnLocation creates its team, and `AllowTeamChangeOnTouch` switches a player to the spawn's team on touch [5][6]. For RP use the Teams service but set `player.Team` from the role picker (not from touching spawns), keep `AutoAssignable = true` only on "Citizen", and spawn returning players at their house by setting their respawn point or moving the character on `CharacterAdded`, as obby checkpoint systems do [7].

| CompleteTask | RemoteEvent | C→S | taskId | task active; player at the station within 10 studs; min task time elapsed | 2 per s |
| TradeRequest / TradeOffer / TradeConfirm | RemoteEvent | C→S | ids | both online; items owned; locked during countdown | 2 per s |

The server owns all money, item ownership, house layouts and trades; the client only asks.

### Luau: claim a house with a prompt (server)
```lua
--!strict
local CollectionService = game:GetService("CollectionService")
local Players = game:GetService("Players")

local HouseService = {}
local plotOf: { [Player]: Model } = {}

local function claim(player: Player, plot: Model)
	if plotOf[player] or plot:GetAttribute("OwnerUserId") ~= nil then return end
	plot:SetAttribute("OwnerUserId", player.UserId)
	plotOf[player] = plot
	HouseService.loadLayout(player, plot) -- clones saved furniture into plot.Furniture
	local door = plot:FindFirstChild("DoorSpawn") :: BasePart?
	local character = player.Character
	if door and character then
		character:PivotTo(door.CFrame * CFrame.new(0, 3, -4))
	end
end

local function bindPlot(inst: Instance)
	if not inst:IsA("Model") then return end
	local prompt = inst:FindFirstChild("ClaimPrompt", true)
	if prompt and prompt:IsA("ProximityPrompt") then
		prompt.HoldDuration = 0.5
		prompt.Triggered:Connect(function(player: Player)
			claim(player, inst)
		end)
	end
end

function HouseService.Start()
	for _, plot in CollectionService:GetTagged("Plot") do bindPlot(plot) end
	CollectionService:GetInstanceAddedSignal("Plot"):Connect(bindPlot)
	Players.PlayerRemoving:Connect(function(player)
		local plot = plotOf[player]
		if not plot then return end
		HouseService.saveLayout(player, plot)
		local furniture = plot:FindFirstChild("Furniture")
		if furniture then furniture:ClearAllChildren() end
		plot:SetAttribute("OwnerUserId", nil)
		plot:SetAttribute("Locked", nil)
		plotOf[player] = nil
	end)
end

function HouseService.plotOf(player: Player): Model?
	return plotOf[player]
end

-- loadLayout / saveLayout serialize { id, x, y, z, r } relative to plot:GetPivot()
function HouseService.loadLayout(_player: Player, _plot: Model) end
function HouseService.saveLayout(_player: Player, _plot: Model) end

return HouseService
```

### Luau: keep intruders out of a locked house (server)
Door locks are social, not security: an exploiter can walk through any wall on their own client. So the server enforces the rule by moving anyone who is inside a locked house without permission back to the front door.
```lua
--!strict
local Players = game:GetService("Players")

local function allowed(plot: Model, player: Player): boolean
	local owner = plot:GetAttribute("OwnerUserId")
	if owner == player.UserId then return true end
	if not plot:GetAttribute("Locked") then return true end
	local ownerPlayer = if typeof(owner) == "number" then Players:GetPlayerByUserId(owner) else nil
	if plot:GetAttribute("FriendsOnly") and ownerPlayer then
		local ok, isFriend = pcall(function() return player:IsFriendsWith(ownerPlayer.UserId) end)
		return ok and isFriend == true
	end
	return false
end

local function sweep(plot: Model)
	local bounds = plot:FindFirstChild("Bounds") :: BasePart?
	local door = plot:FindFirstChild("DoorSpawn") :: BasePart?
	if not bounds or not door then return end
	local params = OverlapParams.new()
	params.FilterType = Enum.RaycastFilterType.Include
	local characters = {}
	for _, p in Players:GetPlayers() do
		if p.Character then table.insert(characters, p.Character) end
	end
	params.FilterDescendantsInstances = characters
	local seen: { [Player]: boolean } = {}
	for _, part in workspace:GetPartBoundsInBox(bounds.CFrame, bounds.Size, params) do
		local model = part:FindFirstAncestorOfClass("Model")
		local player = model and Players:GetPlayerFromCharacter(model)
		if player and not seen[player] then
			seen[player] = true
			if not allowed(plot, player) then
				(model :: Model):PivotTo(door.CFrame * CFrame.new(0, 3, 6)) -- outside, facing the door
			end
		end
	end
end
-- Run sweep(plot) every 1 s for locked plots only.
```

### Luau: filtered RP names (server)
All player-written text shown to others (RP names, bios, house names, signs) must be filtered before display.
```lua
--!strict
local TextService = game:GetService("TextService")

local function filterForEveryone(text: string, fromUserId: number): string?
	if #text == 0 or #text > 60 then return nil end
	local ok, result = pcall(function()
		return TextService:FilterStringAsync(text, fromUserId)
	end)
	if not ok then return nil end
	local ok2, filtered = pcall(function()
		return (result :: TextFilterResult):GetNonChatStringForBroadcastAsync()
	end)
	if not ok2 then return nil end
	return filtered
end
```
If filtering fails, show nothing (or the player's display name), never the raw text. Re-filter saved names when they load on a new server, because filter rules change.

### Luau: one vehicle per player (server)
```lua
--!strict
local ServerStorage = game:GetService("ServerStorage")
local Players = game:GetService("Players")

local current: { [Player]: Model } = {}
local lastUsed: { [Model]: number } = {}

local function despawn(player: Player)
	local v = current[player]
	if v then
		lastUsed[v] = nil
		v:Destroy()
		current[player] = nil
	end
end

local function spawnVehicle(player: Player, vehicleId: string)
	local template = ServerStorage.Vehicles:FindFirstChild(vehicleId)
	local character = player.Character
	local root = character and character:FindFirstChild("HumanoidRootPart") :: BasePart?
	if not template or not root then return end
	despawn(player)
	local vehicle = (template :: Model):Clone()
	vehicle:PivotTo(root.CFrame * CFrame.new(8, 2, 0))
	vehicle:SetAttribute("OwnerUserId", player.UserId)
	vehicle.Parent = workspace.Vehicles -- parent last
	current[player] = vehicle
	lastUsed[vehicle] = os.clock()
	local seat = vehicle:FindFirstChildWhichIsA("VehicleSeat", true)
	if seat then
		seat:GetPropertyChangedSignal("Occupant"):Connect(function()
			lastUsed[vehicle] = os.clock()
		end)
	end
end

Players.PlayerRemoving:Connect(despawn)

task.spawn(function()
	while true do
		task.wait(30)
		for player, v in current do
			local seat = v:FindFirstChildWhichIsA("VehicleSeat", true)
			local occupied = seat and (seat :: VehicleSeat).Occupant ~= nil
			if not occupied and os.clock() - (lastUsed[v] or 0) > 300 then
				despawn(player) -- idle 5 minutes
			end
		end
	end
end)
```
Vehicles must be driven by physics (constraints, VehicleSeat), never by tweens; tweened parts don't carry riders [19].

### Luau: validate a furniture placement (server)
```lua
--!strict
local GRID = 0.5
local MAX_ITEMS = 400

local function snap(n: number): number
	return math.floor(n / GRID + 0.5) * GRID
end

local function validPlacement(plot: Model, size: Vector3, cf: CFrame, count: number): (boolean, CFrame?)
	if count >= MAX_ITEMS then return false, nil end
	local bounds = plot:FindFirstChild("Bounds") :: BasePart?
	if not bounds then return false, nil end
	local localCf = bounds.CFrame:ToObjectSpace(cf)
	local _, yaw, _ = localCf:ToOrientation()
	local yawSnapped = math.rad(math.floor(math.deg(yaw) / 15 + 0.5) * 15)
	local pos = Vector3.new(snap(localCf.X), snap(localCf.Y), snap(localCf.Z))
	local half = bounds.Size / 2
	local r = math.max(size.X, size.Z) / 2
	if math.abs(pos.X) + r > half.X or math.abs(pos.Z) + r > half.Z then return false, nil end
	if pos.Y < -half.Y or pos.Y + size.Y > half.Y then return false, nil end
	return true, bounds.CFrame * CFrame.new(pos) * CFrame.Angles(0, yawSnapped, 0)
end
```
The server re-snaps every position, checks bounds and count, charges money, then clones the model and parents it last.

## 12. Twists that work

1. **RP + a real job minigame.** Each job is a 20-60 s skill toy (stack burgers, sort mail, fix a car) instead of standing still.
2. **RP + pets or a companion** that follows you, needs care and grows (the pet-sim layer on a town).
3. **RP + fantasy or genre setting.** Magic school, space station, underwater town, superhero city, medieval village. Same systems, new props.
4. **RP + a story spine.** Weekly "town events" (a storm, a festival, a mystery) give servers a shared scene; see [[Genre - Story and Puzzle]].
5. **RP + building.** A real build mode makes the house the long-term goal (life sim).
6. **RP + fashion.** Outfit contests in the town square on a timer; see [[Genre - Party and Social Hangout]].
7. **RP + incidents.** The game generates calls (fire, robbery, sick NPC) so emergency roles always have work.
8. **RP + seasons.** Snow in winter, a beach festival in summer: the town changes, so regulars notice.
9. **RP + creator props.** Players design signs, posters or menus from shapes and filtered text.
10. **Small-scale RP.** One building done to perfection (a hotel, a hospital, a cruise ship) instead of a thin town.

## 13. Failure modes

1. **An empty town.** RP needs people; a server with 2 players has no scenes. Fix: server size 20-30, a small dense town, NPCs and incidents that give solo players something to do.
2. **No starting idea.** Players arrive, look around and leave. Fix: scene starter cards, a role picker, interactive props in the first room.
3. **Grief.** Vehicle ramming, trapping, following, house invasions, spam. Fix: no player-vehicle collisions at low speed in the plaza, house locks enforced by the server, block and kick tools, abilities that can't target others.
4. **Unfiltered text.** RP names, bios, signs or house names shown raw. This is a platform rules violation.
5. **Inappropriate RP.** Romantic or adult scenes. Fix: no props or passes that suggest it, report hooks, quick server tools for private-server owners.
6. **Lost houses.** A layout that fails to save or loads at the wrong offset. Store relative positions and a version number.
7. **Lag.** Hundreds of vehicles, thousands of furniture parts, unanchored props. Fix: caps, despawns, anchored furniture, streaming.
8. **Phone-hostile build mode.** Fix: tap-select, grid snap, big buttons.
9. **Inflation and dead trading.** No sinks, duplicates with no use. Fix: sinks, combining.
10. **Paywalled basics.** Only one free house or vehicle. Fix: 6+ free of each.
11. **Thin updates.** A recolor every month. Fix: a new place or system each 2-4 weeks.

## Common mistakes

1. Showing RP names or bios without filtering them.
2. Trusting the client's "I completed a task" or "I placed this item here" without checks.
3. Saving furniture in world coordinates, so layouts break when the player claims a different plot.
4. Letting each player spawn unlimited vehicles.
5. Tweening vehicles, elevators or boats so riders slide off [19].
6. Door locks enforced only by CanCollide.
7. Paid abilities that let players grief.
8. A huge empty town with props only in the plaza.
9. A role picker with 30 roles and no uniforms or tools.
10. A build mode that needs a mouse.
11. No block or kick tools for private-server owners.
12. Two scripts setting `ProcessReceipt` [10].
13. Wages paid per minute online (AFK farming).
14. A trade window without a countdown and a final confirm.
15. Limited items that come back without warning.
16. Needs that punish (fainting, losing items) instead of nudging.
17. Romantic props or passes, which break Roblox's rules.
18. Forgetting to clear a leaving player's furniture and plot ownership.
